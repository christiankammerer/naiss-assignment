**Observations:**
- **W_v.0 has a sharp 30-dimensional subspace.** Its singular values run evenly from 1.765 down to about 1.09 for 30 values (with ln1_w.0 folded in, 1.47 down to 0.905). Then they drop to 0.829, 0.759, 0.709 and a random-noise bulk of about 0.6 or less. The update to W_v.0 can only lie in the span of the layer-norm-0 inputs, so this subspace should be the character-embedding space.
- **Content and position heads split cleanly.** In layer 0, heads 0 and 4 keep 33–49% of their Q/K energy inside this subspace (random would be 7.8%). Heads 1, 2 and 3 keep only about 5%, so they are position heads. W_fc.0 keeps 24%.
- **Directions 0–28 are plain character directions.** The content heads read them at 4–9× the random rate, W_fc.0 at 2–5×, and the position heads avoid them (about 0.5×).
- **Direction 29 is a character that heads attend to.** The position heads read it at 7.5× the random rate. It is the top input direction of W_k.0 (cos 0.86, singular value 3.23), and it is partly aligned with the very large top singular directions of W_k.1, W_k.2 and W_k.3 (5.30, 4.70, 3.68). The queries that match it come 80–91% from the character subspace. That fits a frequent character used as an attention target, such as the space at a word boundary, rather than a pure position direction.
- **After the 30 strong directions come about 4 weak ones (indices 30–33).** The content heads read them at only 1.1–1.75× the random rate, and after that it is noise. That fits characters the model saw only rarely.

**Reasoning:** The model seems to use about 30 characters heavily plus about 3–4 rarely, roughly 33–34 in total. Normalized Python code has only 28 common symbols (newline, space, a–z), and å/ä/ö/æ/ø almost never appear, so I would expect 28 strong directions and about 5 near-dead ones. The Scandinavian texts have about 31 frequent characters (newline, space, a–z and three native vowels). They also have a few rare ones: q, and the neighbouring languages' letters (æ/ø in Swedish, ä/ö in Danish and Norwegian), which show up in names. "30 strong + a few weak" fits those texts better than Python, though not decisively. One of the 30 might instead carry position information, and then the count would match Python's 28. I found nothing in the weights that tells Swedish, Norwegian and Danish apart, because all three have the same kind of character inventory. Choosing Swedish is close to a guess, based on its slightly different set of rare letters.
